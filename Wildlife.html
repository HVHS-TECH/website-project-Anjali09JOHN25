Kangaroo

Kangaroo is one of the most iconic animals in Australia and is commonly found in bushland, grasslands, and open areas around the country, including parts of Queensland. They are known for their powerful hind legs, strong tails, and ability to hop at high speeds across long distances. Kangaroos usually live in groups called mobs and are most active during the early morning and evening when it is cooler. They feed mainly on grasses and plants and are well adapted to Australia’s dry and varied environments.

Tasmanian Devil

Tasmanian devil is a carnivorous marsupial native to Tasmania and is known for its strong jaws and loud screeching calls. They are mainly scavengers, feeding on carrion, but can also hunt small animals. Despite their small size, they are very strong and play an important role in their ecosystem by helping clean up animal remains. Tasmanian devils are mostly nocturnal and prefer forested and coastal scrub environments.

Wombat

Wombat is a sturdy, burrowing marsupial found in forests, grasslands, and mountainous regions of Australia. It has a strong body, short legs, and powerful claws that allow it to dig extensive tunnel systems underground. Wombats are mostly nocturnal and feed on grasses, roots, and other vegetation. They are solitary animals and spend much of their time in their burrows, which help protect them from predators and harsh weather conditions.

Platypus

Platypus is one of the most unusual animals in the world, found in freshwater rivers and streams in eastern Australia. It has a duck-like bill, webbed feet, and a beaver-like tail, making it look like a mix of several animals. Platypuses are excellent swimmers and use their sensitive bill to detect food like insects and small aquatic creatures underwater. They are shy, mostly nocturnal, and spend much of their time in burrows near riverbanks.

Koala

Koala is a tree-dwelling marsupial that lives mainly in eucalyptus forests along Australia’s east coast. They are known for their round ears, fluffy grey fur, and slow-moving lifestyle. Koalas spend most of their time resting in trees and feeding on eucalyptus leaves, which provide most of their hydration and energy. They are highly adapted to climbing and gripping branches, making forests an essential part of their survival and habitat.

Echidna

Short-beaked echidna is a unique Australian mammal covered in spines, often found in forests, grasslands, and even urban areas. It uses its long, sticky tongue to feed on ants and termites, which make up most of its diet. Echidnas are shy and slow-moving animals that spend much of their time foraging on the ground. They are also one of the few egg-laying mammals in the world, making them especially unusual and scientifically interesting.

Cockatoo

Cockatoo is a highly intelligent and social bird commonly found across Australia, including forests, woodlands, and urban areas. They are known for their distinctive crests, loud calls, and strong beaks, which they use to crack seeds and nuts. Cockatoos often travel in flocks and are very expressive and playful, sometimes forming strong bonds with humans in urban environments. Their adaptability allows them to thrive in a wide range of habitats.
